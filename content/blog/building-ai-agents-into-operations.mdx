Every company we talk to is "doing AI." Few are getting ROI. The difference is usually not the model — it's whether the agent is attached to a real workflow with real data and real guardrails.

## Start with a workflow, not a model

The question isn't "which LLM?" It's "what does your team repeat that a machine could do well enough, fast enough, and safely enough?"

The best candidates share three traits:

1. **High volume.** The workflow happens dozens of times a day.
2. **Well-documented.** There's a playbook, policy or FAQ to ground the agent in.
3. **Judgement-light.** The answer doesn't require creative leaps, just correct retrieval and formatting.

Support triage, lead qualification, invoice matching and claims intake all qualify.

## Ground it in your data

A raw chat model hallucinates. A retrieval pipeline grounded in your documentation, tickets and policies mostly doesn't. We build RAG pipelines with:

- Your actual policies and playbooks as the source of truth
- Chunking and embeddings tuned to your document types
- Citations in every answer, so humans can verify
- Evaluation sets that catch regressions before they ship

## Guardrails make it production-ready

Autonomous doesn't mean unsupervised. Every agent we ship gets:

- A confidence threshold — below it, escalate to a human
- Action boundaries — what it can do on its own vs. what needs approval
- Full audit trails on everything it touches
- A kill switch and rate limits

## Measure it like a feature

We instrument agents like any product feature. Resolution rate, escalation rate, cost per resolution, latency. If an agent isn't measurably better than the status quo on those numbers, we tell you and we iterate until it is.

AI that ships is boring on purpose. The agent just quietly resolves 70% of your tickets, and your team finally works on interesting problems.
